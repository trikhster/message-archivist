Let’s set up your message backups

Your messages, saved on your own computer, ready to read here or put back on an Android phone. The quickest start is from your phone: years of texts in minutes. Then this extension keeps the backup up to date.Then the extension, Message Archivist for Chrome or Edge, can keep it up to date from Google Messages, in the same folder.
  1. Choose a folder for your backups. We suggest making a new folder called “Messages Backup” in your Documents folder. Chrome will ask you to allow the extension to save there: choose Allow, or Allow on every visit if it offers that.If you already use the extension, choose the folder it saves to. Your browser asks you to allow the app to save there: choose Allow on every visit if it offers that.
  2. Start with your phone (fastest). On your Android phone, open the Message Archivist app, tap Back up this phone and save the file. It reads the phone’s own copy of your texts, so years of them take minutes instead of hours. Copy the file to this computer (or open it from Google Drive), then bring it in. How to get the app
    No Android phone to hand? Skip this; the next steps back everything up from Google Messages for Web instead, which takes longer.No Android phone to hand? Back up from Google Messages for Web with the extension instead (the next step), which takes longer.
  3. Open Google Messages and connect your phone. On your phone, open Messages, tap your profile picture → Device pairing → QR code scanner, and scan the code shown in Chrome.
  4. Back up everything from here once, then keep it up to date. This fills in what the phone doesn’t keep (some chat messages you sent, during a Google Messages bug), contact cards and pictures, and matches the messages already brought in from the phone instead of saving them twice. After that, only new messages are added: by hand, or on a schedule (Backups tab). Google Messages moves into a small window in the corner of your screen while it works, so you can keep using your computer; just don’t minimise or cover it.
  5. Keep Google Messages backed up (optional). Message Archivist for Chrome, the extension, backs up Google Messages for Web into the same folder, photos and all, and fills in what a phone backup can miss. Install it in Chrome or Edge from the releases page; this app shows what it saves.

Your backups

“Back up everything” goes through every conversation in Google Messages, one after another. Years of texts can take hours the first time, and your phone needs to stay on and connected; a phone backup from the Message Archivist app gets you there in minutes (Tools → Bring in files…), and this then only adds what’s new. You can stop it any time; next time it carries on where it left off.
Backing up from Google Messages is done by the extension, Message Archivist for Chrome or Edge, into this same folder. Here you read and search your archive, bring in phone backups and other apps’ chats, and make files to put texts back on a phone.

Every chat

Each chat in your backup or on your automatic backup list, how complete it is, and its photos. Click a chat for the details. Choose how often it’s backed up by itself in its row.

Put your texts back on an Android phone

Makes one file with every chat that has a phone number, its messages in order, and its photos and videos. Copy it to the phone and open it with the Message Archivist app (part of this project): it puts the texts back into the phone’s messages, then hands back to Google Messages. Times are to the minute; messages sent in the same minute keep their order. RCS chats go back as ordinary texts and picture messages. iPhones can’t import texts.
Using SMS Backup & Restore instead?
The same messages as an XML file for the SMS Backup & Restore app (SyncTech). Photos are written inside the file as text, so it’s about a third bigger.

Bring in conversations

Add to your archive: a phone backup from the Message Archivist app (the .tar it saved, and its updates file if you use one), chats exported from WhatsApp, Telegram, Discord, Instagram, Messenger, TikTok or Snapchat, or exports from earlier versions of this extension. Pick one or several; each is recognised by itself, and only what isn’t here yet is added. The files you pick aren’t changed.
Where each file comes from

    Obsidian

    Your archive as notes in Obsidian: one for every person and every chat, linked together, each person's with what you talk about (words and phrases), the moments and events of your chats and who they're connected to; a calendar of every day, the events found in your messages (birthdays, milestones, travel, meeting up), every photo by day, and the messages a year at a time with photos and videos shown where they were sent. They go in an Obsidian folder inside your backup folder, so they never leave your computer. In Obsidian, choose Open folder as vault and pick the backup folder itself. Anything you write below the “Your notes” line of a person or chat note is kept when the notes are updated.
