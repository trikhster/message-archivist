# Privacy policy

Message Archivist's Chrome extension runs entirely in your browser.

- **No data leaves your computer.** The extension has no server, analytics, tracking or advertising. It never sends your messages, contacts, photos or settings anywhere.
- **What it reads:** only the messages.google.com tab you're using, and only while a backup, analysis or scheduled job runs. That includes each chat's Details page: participants' names, numbers and contact photos, and whether the chat is end-to-end encrypted.
- **What it writes:**
  - files in the backup folder you choose and allow access to (messages, photos, each chat's list of photos, contact cards, logs, checksums), including pieces saved during a run (`journal/`) that are removed once they're folded in
  - date-range copies you ask for, downloaded as zip files
  - its own settings and run history (including run logs), kept in Chrome's local extension storage on your computer
- **Other apps' exports:** when you bring in an export from WhatsApp, Telegram, Discord, Instagram, Messenger, TikTok or Snapchat, the extension reads the file or folder you pick, on your computer, and copies its chats and media into your backup folder. It never signs in to those apps or contacts them; it remembers which name is yours in each app (in Chrome's local extension storage) so it needn't ask again.
- **Snapchat for Web** (off unless you turn it on in Settings → Apps, which asks Chrome to let the extension read snapchat.com): while it's on, the extension reads the chats you open at snapchat.com/web, as you read them: their names, who wrote each message, its words and when, and that a photo or video was there (not the photo itself). It only reads the page: it never clicks, types or changes anything there, never opens a Snap, never saves anything in Snapchat and never takes a screenshot, so nobody is notified of anything. What it reads stays in the extension's own storage on your computer until you choose **Put them in my archive**, which adds it to your backup folder and removes it from there. Turning it off stops it and gives back the permission.
- **What it opens:** "Open my backup" reads your backup folder to show it, and saves the labels, flags and notes you add there (`labels.json`) and who's who in its People tab (`people.json`); nothing is uploaded. "Check every photo" in Settings only reads your backup folder. "Make the restore file" writes a file for SMS Backup & Restore into your backup folder; it only goes to a phone if you copy it there.
- **The Android app** reads only the restore file you choose and writes its messages into the phone's messages. It has no internet permission and sends nothing anywhere. While it restores it is your SMS app (Android requires that to add messages); it saves texts that arrive meanwhile to your inbox, reads the phone's own number (for group chats) and shows notifications. It keeps nothing after the restore. When you **back up the phone** with it, it reads the phone's texts and picture messages (and, if you allow it, your contacts for the chats in the backup: names, whether they're starred, their photos, and the rest of each contact that helps the archive tell who's who and fill in its notes: their other numbers and email addresses, nickname, company and job title, birthday, contact labels and relations; not their postal address or the notes on the contact; with **Save all my contacts** on in its Settings, off unless you turn it on, the same for every contact with a number or email address, not only those in the backup's chats, so your archive can tie other apps' chats to them) and writes them into the file you choose; that file stays wherever you save or share it. Backing up doesn't make it your SMS app. If you turn on automatic backup (**Back up automatically** after a backup, or **Back up by itself** in its Settings), it does the same in the background as often as you choose (from every hour to every 30 days), into the file you chose (if that's on Google Drive, the Drive app uploads it; the app itself still has no internet access, and only checks whether it's on Wi-Fi when you ask it to wait for Wi-Fi). For a file on Google Drive it also keeps a copy of that file in its own storage on the phone, which no other app can read, so it needn't download the file each time; it's left out of Android's backup of the app, turning off **Keep a copy on this phone** in Settings deletes it, and so does uninstalling the app. If you bring such a file into the extension (Settings → Tools), the extension reads it on your computer and adds its messages and photos to your backup folder; nothing is uploaded.
- **Exports you share to the Android app** (a WhatsApp chat, a Snapchat, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok or Discord download, or a file you add in an app's section) are copied into the app's own storage, which no other app can read, until your next backup puts them into the backup file you chose; then those copies are deleted, and only a note of what went where (the export's name, size and file) stays. **Remove** deletes one that's still waiting. The app reads the files' names, and a zip's list of files, to tell which app an export is from; it doesn't send them anywhere.
- **The app** (Message Archivist installed from Chrome or Edge, at trikhster.github.io/message-archivist/app): its pages are static files served by GitHub Pages from this repository's releases. Loading them is an ordinary visit to a GitHub website (GitHub sees the request, as for any site; see GitHub's privacy statement); after that the app keeps its own copy and opens offline. It reads and writes only the backup folder you choose, keeps its settings and run history in the browser's storage for that app on your computer, and sends nothing anywhere: a content security policy stops its pages connecting to anything but their own address. Your messages never go to GitHub.
- **Notes for Obsidian** (Settings → Tools → Obsidian, off unless you use it) are written into an `Obsidian` folder in your backup folder, from what's already there; the extension doesn't talk to Obsidian or send them anywhere. If you sync that folder (Obsidian Sync, iCloud Drive, Dropbox…), that service uploads it, as it would the rest of the folder.
- **Deleting a chat** from the backup moves its files into `removed-chats/` in your backup folder; delete that folder to remove them for good. Google Messages and your phone aren't touched.
- **Windows:** to keep working while you use Chrome, a backup can move the Google Messages tab into its own window (and, if you choose, make that window small and move it to a corner of the screen), and a scheduled backup can open Google Messages in a window and close it afterwards.
- **Logs:** each run's log, kept in the backup folder and in Settings → Past runs, includes chat names, contact names and numbers, times and file names, but not message text. "Copy log" only copies it to your clipboard; you decide whether to share it. Remove anything private before posting it publicly.
- **Diagnostics:** "Copy page diagnostics" copies the page's structure to your clipboard with all message text masked. It isn't sent anywhere.

## Permissions and why they're needed

| Permission | Used for |
|---|---|
| Access to messages.google.com | Reading the conversation you're backing up or analysing. |
| Access to googleusercontent.com, google.com, googleapis.com, gstatic.com | Downloading the photos, videos and map previews Messages shows in the chat. |
| Access to other websites (**optional**, off unless you turn it on in Settings) | Downloading the pictures in link previews (Spotify, TikTok, news sites…). Used only for those pictures. |
| Access to snapchat.com (**optional**, off unless you turn on Snapchat for Web in Settings → Apps) | Reading the chats you open on Snapchat for Web, as you read them (read only). Given back when you turn it off. |
| storage, unlimitedStorage | Saving your settings, schedules and run history locally. |
| downloads | Putting date-range zip files into the Downloads subfolder you choose. |
| alarms | Checking every 15 minutes whether a scheduled backup is due. |
| notifications | Telling you when a backup finishes, if you turn that on. |
| scripting, activeTab | Starting the exporter on a Messages tab that was open before the extension was installed or reloaded, and stopping that page opening new tabs while a backup runs. |

Questions or concerns: [open an issue](https://github.com/trikhster/message-archivist/issues) on GitHub. Please don't include message content or contact details.
