# Obsidian notes (`extension/obsidian.js`)

The archive as an Obsidian vault: open the backup folder itself in Obsidian ("Open folder as vault") and every person, chat, month and topic is a note, with the messages in notes of their own, linked together, and photos and videos embedded from the chats' own `media/` folders (never copied). `GMObsidian`, plain functions over an `io` (`{ readText, write, remove? }`) like `GMLib` ([[Library module]]), so Settings, a backup run and `test/people/run.mjs` share them. Everything is counted and matched in the extension: no model, nothing sent anywhere ([[Decisions]]).

## Where it runs
- **Settings → Tools → Obsidian** ([[Settings page]]): *Write the notes* / *Update the notes* (`writeObsidian`), and the switch **Keep them up to date after every backup and import** (setting `obsidian`, [[Storage and settings]]). Turning it on writes them at once, there, where it's quickest.
- **Delete the notes…** (Settings → Tools → Obsidian, shown once there are notes) deletes the whole `Obsidian/` folder, in two steps in the `obsidianDlg` dialog: first what goes (every file in it, how many notes the owner wrote in (`hasYours` on the `ownable` notes in the manifest: people's, chats', topics', and the calendar's years and months), and files of their own not in the manifest), that it isn't moved anywhere and can't be undone, and that the notes would have to be written again; then **Continue…** to a last plain warning and **Delete them for good**. It refuses while a backup is running in the Google Messages tab, turns the switch off (so a backup doesn't write them back), then `removeEntry('Obsidian', { recursive: true })`. In the app it says the extension's switch is its own.
- **After every Settings run** that rewrites the index (imports, deletes, clean-ups): `finishLibraryRun` brings them up to date when the switch is on, with a line in the run's log.
- **After every backup from Google Messages** ([[Backup run]]): `writeIndex` in `content.js` does the same at the end of a final run, through the writer frame (`sink.writeTemp`, so the notes stay out of the checksums), and logs "✓ Obsidian notes: …". `obsidian.js` is a content script for this.

## What it writes (`Obsidian/` in the backup folder)
```
Obsidian/
  Message Archivist.md                         home: start here, lately, years, people, topics, sources
  People.md, People/<name>.md                  everyone (most recent, lost touch, A–Z, one-way); each person
  People/<name>/<name> timeline.md             everything notable with them, year by year
  People/<name>/<name> photos.md, … links.md   what was shared in their one-to-one chats
  Calendar.md, Calendar/<YYYY>.md              every year; each year
  Calendar/<YYYY>/<YYYY-MM>.md                 each month, day by day
  Events.md                                    birthdays, milestones, travel, photo days, holidays, meeting up
  Photos.md, Photos/<YYYY> photos.md           every photo and video, by day
  Photos/<YYYY>/<YYYY-MM> photos.md            a month of them, in a year of more than 300
  Topics.md, Topics/<topic>.md                 words and phrases two people (or groups) or more share
  Labels.md, Labels/<label>.md                 the labels in the phone's contacts, and who has them
  Chats/<source>.md, Chats/<source>/<chat>.md  each source's chats A–Z; each chat
  Chats/<source>/<chat>/<chat> <YYYY>.md       a year of a chat's messages
  Chats/<source>/<chat>/<chat> <YYYY-MM>.md    a month, in a busy year
  Chats/<source>/<chat>/<chat> undated.md      messages with no date
  .message-archivist.json                      the manifest (hidden in Obsidian)
```
- **`<source>`** is the chat folder's app folder (`WhatsApp`, `Google Messages - (555) 019-9999`), or *Google Messages* for the main texts; **`<chat>`** its folder's name. People are named by their People name ([[Sources]] → People); two with one name get ` (<app>)`, then ` (2)`. Names lose `\ / : * ? " < > | # ^ [ ]` (`noteName`), which file systems or Obsidian links can't hold, and Windows' reserved names (`con`, `nul`…) get `_`. Photo notes end in ` photos` so no two notes share a name with a calendar year.
- **How a chat's messages are split:** a chat of 100 messages or fewer (`small`) has them in its own note, under `## Messages`; a longer one gets a note a year, and a year of more than 400 (`busyYear`) a note a month. So years of a few texts a year don't make piles of one-message notes. Each period note links back to the chat and to the next and previous ones.
- **Frontmatter** for Obsidian's properties and Dataview: people (`type: person`, `aliases`: their other names, nickname, numbers and emails; `numbers`, `emails`, `handles`, `sources`, `messages`, `sent`, `received`, `conversations`, `they_start` (a percentage), `first`, `last`, `birthday`, `topics` and `connections` as links, and from the contacts `starred`, `nickname`, `organization`, `job_title`, `labels`); topics (`type: topic`, `people`, `chats`, `mentions`, `first`, `last`); years (`type: year`, `year`, `messages`, `people`, `photos`) and months (`type: month`, `month`, `messages`, `photos`, `people` as links); timelines (`type: timeline`), photos (`type: photos` with `year` or `month`, and `type: media` for a person's), links (`type: links`), each with `person` where it's someone's; chats (`type: chat`, `source`, `account`, `person` or `people` as links, `messages`, `first`, `last`, `tags` from the chat's labels, `folder`); periods (`type: messages`, `source`, `chat`, `person`, `year` or `month`, `first`, `last`, `messages`). Values are JSON, which is valid YAML.
- **Links** are wikilinks with the path from the vault's root (`[[Obsidian/People/Sam Rivers|Sam Rivers]]`), so they never depend on a name being unique. A day is a link to its heading (`[[…/Sam Rivers 2024-03#Saturday, 2 March 2024|2 March 2024]]`): in the messages (the period note, or the chat note for a short chat; `dayLink`), in a month note, or in a photos note. `dayHeading` makes every such heading and every link to one, so they can't drift apart. **In a table** a link's `|` is written `\|` (`cell`), or Obsidian starts a new column there ([[Gotchas]]). Media are `![[conversations/<chat>/media/<file>|320]]` (images 320 px wide; 200 in the photo notes), or, when the file name has a character a wikilink can't hold, a Markdown link relative to the note (`../../../../conversations/…`, percent-encoded). Not saved: `*(photo not saved: <why>)*`.
- **A message:** `**Sender** · 14:05` (`≈` before a time from the date heading; no time when only the day is known; ` · *edited*`, ` · ★` when flagged), a reply as `*↳ Sam: “…”*`, the text, the media, reactions in italics, and a flag's note (`labels.json` flags of chats with an ID). Days are `## Saturday, 1 March 2025` (`###` inside a chat note). Calls and notices are a line in italics.
- **Text reads literally** (`inline`, `lines`): `\ ` * _ [ ] < > # $ ^ | &` get a backslash, and doubled `= ~ %` (highlights, strikeouts, comments) too; web addresses are left as they are so they stay links; each line loses its leading spaces and a leading `-`, `+` or `1.` is escaped, so no message becomes a list, heading, rule or code block.

## What a chat says (`analyse(msgs, periods)`)
Worked out once, when a chat is read, for every chat (people's and groups'), and kept in the manifest as the chat's `a`; `merge` adds a person's chats up. In one pass: sent and received, calls, media (`kinds`: photos `p`, videos `v`, voice notes `a`, other files `f`; `files`: the newest 10,000 saved ones as `[file, day, me, kind]`; `mdays`: photos and videos per day), per year `[sent, received]`, hours, weekdays, reply-time histograms each way, conversations (a new one after a pause of six hours (`GAP`), or on a new day when untimed) and who started them, the longest, the first message, messages per day (`days`), who said what in a group (`senders`: the top 25, by number when there is one, else by name), the top 60 capitalised mid-sentence words (`names`, for mentions), the top 8 emoji each way, the newest 300 links, and the events (below). Then a second pass over the most used **words and phrases**: `terms` keeps, for the top 100 words (2 uses up) and top 40 phrases (3 up), `[uses, first day, last day, the day most, uses that day]`, and `yterms` each year's top 12. Days in `terms`, `events` and `files` are day numbers (days since 1970, `dayNum`/`numDay`); in `days` and `mdays`, dates.

**Words and phrases** (`termsIn`): a word says something when it's 3 to 24 letters, not a stopword (`STOP`: common words, chat filler, greetings, weekdays and months), not a number, not laughter (`hahaha`) and not drawn out (`sooo`); a phrase is two or three such words in a row (`dog park`, `fantasy football league`), or two around a small word (`JOIN`: `game of thrones`), never across punctuation. What each word is is remembered (`MEANT`), since words repeat.

**Events** (`dayEvents`), from everything each side said on a day, as `[day, kind, me, hint]`: `bday` (happy birthday, hbd, many happy returns, and a few languages'; `hint` `belated`, or in a group the capitalised name after it, "Happy birthday Cleo!"), `anniv`, `congrats` (congratulations, mazel tov, so proud of you…; `hint` a guess at what for from the day's words: engagement, wedding, baby, graduation, new job, new home, exams, retirement), `holiday` (Christmas, New Year, Lunar New Year, Thanksgiving, Easter, Diwali, Eid, Hanukkah, Mother's and Father's Day, Valentine's Day, Halloween), `trip` (just landed, safe travels, boarding now, layover…; `hint` the place after "landed in", "trip to"…), `meet` (on my way, omw, I'm outside, pulling up, running late, be there in…). `me` is 1 when you said it. Nothing about illness or loss is looked for. A **photo day** isn't stored: it's a chat's day with 5 photos or videos or more (`PHOTO_DAY`), from `mdays`.

## A person's note
The place to start, built from their one-to-one chats:
- **The gist** in a `> [!summary]` callout: messages and dates, your share, how fast each of you usually replies (the median of `REPLY` buckets, from 5 replies up), who starts conversations, then **Talks about** (their words) and **Phrases**, linked when shared.
- **From the phone's contacts** (`contactOf`: the person's entry in the address book (`book`, `contacts.json`), then the cards of their chats, the busiest first; `card` comes from a phone backup): their photo (`![[…/contact/…|120]]`) under the title, then a line: ⭐ Starred, “nickname”, job title at organization, birthday (or a likely one, below), labels (links to `Labels/`). Relations are under Connections (**In your contacts**: `sister: [[…]]`), linked when the name is one person's in People.
- **Chats**, then **Moments**, each a link to its day: first message (and from whom), busiest day, longest conversation (10 messages up), longest streak (3 days up), longest silence (30 days up, and the day it ended), most recent, how many of their birthdays you were in touch on, and their timeline. A day is linked in the person's chat with the most messages that day.
- **Events**: a likely birthday (when the contacts have none), how often you probably met up and when last, milestones, travel (with the place), photo days (the biggest), holidays by year.
- **Over the years**: a table, newest first, each year linked to its note in the year's busiest chat, with the share from you, what you talked about that year (`byYear` over their `yterms`: the top 3) and a bar.
- **When you talk**: the part of the day and the busiest weekday, and two sparklines (hours, Monday to Sunday) in code spans so they line up.
- **Connections**: people **named in your chats** with them and people **whose chats name them** (`mentionsOf`: a person's first name, capitalised and not starting a sentence, at least twice; only names no one else has, three letters or more, and not an everyday word (`AMBIG`)); people they're **in groups with** (shared groups from `P.groupsOf`).
- **Shared**: links to the photos and links notes (the newest 400, under their day; links grouped by site), what the media were ("12 photos, 2 videos"), calls, and each side's favourite emoji. Then **Groups**, and `MARK`.

**Their timeline** (`timelineItems`, `timelineMd`; for two-way people with two things or more): the first message, every event (yours with them, and a group's birthday wishes for them), each return after 60 days or more of silence, and each year's busiest day (20 messages up); years newest first, each linked to its calendar year, each date to its day in the messages.

## Topics: words and phrases (`standout`)
Each two-way person's words and phrases (their chats' `terms` added up), and each group chat's of 20 messages or more, weighed by TF-IDF across all of them (`n · ln(1 + N/df)`, a phrase half as much again, being rarer); 3 uses up, no token of anyone's name in People, and, with 6 or more, used by at most half of them. `dedupe` then keeps one of a word and a phrase holding it: a word or shorter phrase with hardly more uses (×1.25) than the longer one goes, a longer phrase round a phrase used more goes, and when they're used about as much the one more of them share stays (so "fantasy football" beats "fantasy football trade" when two people say the first). The top 12 are theirs; one in the top 12 of two or more is a note in `Topics/`: who (people and groups), how often, the first and last day and the day it came up most (each a link), and **related topics** (others two or more of the same people share). `Topics.md` lists them, phrases first. **Each year's** (`byYear` over every person's and group's `yterms`: a year's words weighed against how many years use them, 4 uses up, the top 8) are in the calendar.

## The calendar (`calendarMds`)
From every chat but one-way senders' (`inCal`). `D` is every day: the chats with messages that day and how many, and its photos and videos.
- **A month** (`Calendar/<YYYY>/<YYYY-MM>.md`): messages, people and groups, photos, who most; **Events**, every one that month, same-day ones that say the same thing together (`gather`: "A, B and C wished you happy birthday", Christmas wishes in five chats); then a heading per day (`dayHeading`, so a link can point at it) with each chat's messages that day as a link to that day in it (the ten busiest, then "and N more chats"), the photos as a link to that day in the photo notes (when they show that day: a chat's files past its newest 10,000 aren't kept), and the day's event emoji.
- **A year** (`Calendar/<YYYY>.md`): messages, people, groups, photos and the busiest month; a table of the months; the 10 **people of the year**; **new this year** (whose first message was that year, 20 messages up); the busiest groups; what was **talked about** that year; its **events** (all but meeting up, which is one line: how often and with whom most); the five **busiest days**, each a link to it in its month.
- `Calendar.md`: a table of the years. Years and months end with `MARK`, so the owner can keep a journal in them.

## Events (`eventsMd`)
- **Your birthday**: the day most people wish you happy birthday in one-to-one chats (3 wishes up across the years), and how many each year.
- **Birthdays**, a calendar of them by month: from the contacts, or from the days someone was wished happy birthday (by you in their chat, or by name in a group: `firstNames`, a first name only one two-way person has): *probably* when the same day comes up in two years or more, *maybe* when once. Belated wishes don't count.
- **Milestones** (congratulations and anniversaries), **Travel**, **Photo days** (10 or more in a chat; each a link to the day in the photo notes and in the messages), newest first, 150 (100) at most; **Holidays**: for each, who you exchanged greetings with each year; **Meeting up**: a table of the people you most often "on my way" with, how often, first and latest.

## Photos (`photoMds`)
Every saved photo and video from every chat but one-way senders' (`shots`, from each chat's `files`), by day and then by chat (`**[[Sam]]** · in the messages`): photos as `![[…|200]]` side by side, videos, voice notes and files as links (🎬 🎤 📄). A note a year, or a month in a year of more than 300 (`PHOTO_YEAR`); each links to the next and previous and to its calendar year or month. `Photos.md` lists them.

## Group chats
A group's chat note starts, under its first line, with a callout: your share of the messages, its busiest day (5 messages up), what its media were, **Most active** (each sender's share, linked to their person when their number or name is one of the group's people: `memberOf`), and its **Events**, counted (`eventCounts`). Groups of 20 messages or more are topic documents too, so a topic note can say "Family (group)".

## One-way people
Someone you've never written to (no sent message in any of their chats) is most likely codes, alerts, a business or spam. In a big archive that's most people (live: 647 of 834). They keep their notes, with `one_way: true` and the tag `one-way` (so the graph can leave them out with `-tag:#one-way`) and a line saying so in the gist, but they're left out of topics, mentions, the calendar, events, photos, the home note's busiest people, and People's most recent and lost touch; People lists them in a **One-way** section after A–Z. Only in apps where you've sent something in some chat, since an export that can't tell your messages apart would make everyone one-way; and not when a chat of theirs couldn't be read. `update` returns their number as `oneWay`.

`People.md` lists the 15 most recent people, those you **lost touch** with (50 messages up, none for a year before the archive's newest message, so the note doesn't change by itself as days pass), then A–Z. The home note leads to People, the Calendar, Events, Photos, Topics and Labels, then the last three months and the latest events, the years, the 25 busiest people, the 20 most widely shared topics and the sources.

## Updating (`update(io, { rows, saved, labels, book, full, small, busyYear, onProgress, log })`)
- **People** come from `GMLib.people(rows, saved, book)` with `saved` = `people.json` and `book` = `contacts.json`, so the notes follow the owner's joins, splits and names and the phone's contacts.
- **Only what changed is read and written.** The manifest (version 4) keeps, per chat folder, what the chat says (`a`, above), a signature (count, first, last, `updated_at`, title, the note paths of the chat and its people, its tags and flags, `small`/`busyYear`) and the notes it made, and per note the hash of what was written (`hash`, a 53-bit string hash). An unchanged chat isn't read at all; a note whose text is the same isn't written. A new message rewrites its period's note and its chat's, its person's, its month's and year's, and the index notes, and whatever else what it says changes (other people's topics, events, photos).
- **The owner's notes stay.** Person, chat, topic, year and month notes end with `MARK` (`%% Your notes: anything below this line stays… %%`); on a rewrite, everything below the owner's copy of that line is kept. One whose line was taken out is the owner's now: it's left as it is (counted as `kept`). Period, photo, timeline and index notes are rewritten.
- **A manifest of another version** (`VERSION`, raised whenever what a chat says or how notes are made changes): every chat is read and every note written again, and the old manifest's list of notes is still used for removing, so notes no longer made go. (Version 3 dropped that list, which left the live vault with 1,279 stray notes from version 2; they were removed by hand.)
- **Removing:** notes in the old manifest that aren't made any more (a chat deleted, a person renamed, a chat grown from one note to years, a month with nothing left) are removed, except one with something below the line, or a note without one that the owner edited (its hash differs): those are kept and no longer looked after.
- Returns `{ notes, written, same, removed, kept, people, oneWay, topics, labels, events, suggestions }`; `status(io)` reads the manifest for Settings (`{ updated_at, notes }`).
- **Not in `SHA256SUMS.txt`:** they're derived and the owner may write in them. Settings writes them with a fresh `libraryIO` whose hashes are never merged; a backup run writes them with `sink.writeTemp`.

## Speed
Version 3 and 4, in Node: the live archive (about 146,000 messages, 1,460 chats) makes 2,560 notes in about 1.5 s, with a 1.7 MB manifest; again with nothing new, 0.15 s and one write (the manifest). A made-up archive of 1,500 chats and 460,500 messages of random words (a worst case for phrases) makes 9,500 notes in about 6 s, with an 8.4 MB manifest; again, 0.9 s. Most of a first run is reading words and phrases; caching what each word is (`MEANT`) and each day's number and heading took it from 9.7 s to 6 s.
