# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are recorded here. The format follows
[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/). Versions have four
parts, production.major.minor.patch: the first is 0 until the first production
release, and those releases are published as GitHub pre-releases. The other
three follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/):

- **Major**: changes that break existing backups or need you to redo setup.
- **Minor**: new features, with existing backups still working.
- **Patch**: bug fixes only.

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **One archive for every app.** Bring in your chats from **WhatsApp, Telegram, Discord, Instagram, Messenger and TikTok** from the export each app makes (Settings → Tools → Bring in files…), including Messenger's end-to-end encrypted chats (from Messenger's own download) and a whole folder of WhatsApp chats at once. They sit beside your text messages, each marked with its app, in the same viewer, search and analysis. Bring in a newer export later and only what's new is added. WhatsApp exports from a phone in another language come in right too (their dates, attachments and left-out photos), and a download it recognises but can't read (Facebook's HTML version, one part of a download in several) gets advice on what to download instead.
- **Apps, side by side.** Settings has an **Apps** tab: Google Messages, then a card for each other app with how to get its export, what your archive has from it and when it last came in, and a button to bring more in (and, for iMessage and Signal, why not yet). Behind it, each app is one module of its own, so its name, colour and export steps are the same everywhere: Settings, the viewer, your backup folder's `index.html` and the Android app.
- **Other apps' chats from your phone (Android app).** Below Google Messages, the app has a section for WhatsApp, Messenger, Instagram, TikTok and Discord. Share a chat to Message Archivist from WhatsApp (⋮ → More → **Export chat**), or a download you got on the phone (or **Add a file…** in its section): it goes into your next phone backup, and comes into your archive with it. **Remove** takes back one that's still waiting.
- **Which one is you?** When an export doesn't say whose it is, Settings asks once per app and remembers.
- **Back up as often as you like (Android app).** Automatic backup can run every hour, 6 hours, 12 hours, day or week, or at a custom interval of any number of hours or days (1 hour to 30 days). The switch after a backup is now **Back up automatically**.
- The viewer shows each chat's app, filters the list by app (**All apps**), and links a chat to the same person's chats in other apps (**Also in WhatsApp · Telegram**).
- **People.** A new **People** tab in the viewer puts each person's chats together across apps, accounts and numbers: chats that share a phone number, email address or app username join by themselves; people with the same name are suggested (**Same person** / **Not the same**, or join them all). **Same person as…**, **Not them** and **Rename…** do the rest, saved in your backup folder (`people.json`). A person's page lists their chats and groups and has one timeline of every message between you; each chat's header links to it ("👤 Sam · also in WhatsApp · maybe Instagram?").
- **The app.** Message Archivist installs as an app on your computer from Chrome or Edge ([trikhster.github.io/message-archivist/app](https://trikhster.github.io/message-archivist/app/)): its own window and icon, no browser tabs, and it works offline. It does everything with your archive that the extension's Settings and viewer do (bring in files, People, Obsidian, checks, phone restore files), and brings in a phone backup opened with it from Finder or Explorer. Backing up Google Messages stays in the extension, into the same folder. Published from each release, with every file's SHA-256 listed in the release.
- **Codes and spam kept apart.** Chats of verification codes, promotions and spam, and other automated texts (alerts and deliveries from short codes and business names) are kept out of the viewer's All chats, People, Search, Media and Analysis, in folders of their own (🔑 Codes, 📣 Promotions, 🤖 Automated). **Show them everywhere** puts them back, and **It's not** keeps a chat that was misjudged. Promotions are known by the opt-out lines marketing texts carry, however they're spelled or capitalised ("Reply STOP", "STOP=end", "STOP2END", "Rply STOP", "HELP4help", "Msg&data rates may apply"), and links with sale or scam words from numbers you haven't saved. Groups, labelled chats and chats you've talked in are never caught.
- **Back up only some chats (Android app).** *Back up this phone* has a **Chats** card: choose which chats go in, from a list with search, **Select all**, each chat's size, and a sort (newest or oldest first, most or fewest messages, largest or smallest, name). Chips filter it by type, **Conversations**, **Groups**, **Codes**, **Promotions & spam** and **Automated**, each with how many; a type you hide isn't backed up, and the kinds Settings leaves out start hidden. A new file holds just the chats chosen; updating a backup brings just those up to date and leaves its other chats as they were. Automatic backups are always of every chat.
- **Leave them out of phone backups (Android app).** Settings → *Leave out of phone backups* has the same three switches: those chats aren't written into any backup, by hand or automatic, and a backup made before drops them at its next update. Groups, starred contacts and chats you've talked in are always kept. The done screen says how many were left out.
- **Leave them out of your archive** (Settings): tick verification codes, promotions and spam, or other automated texts, and "Back up everything" and imports skip them. **Find the ones already in my archive…** moves those already saved into `removed-chats`, after showing the list.
- **Notes for Obsidian.** Settings → Tools → **Obsidian** writes your archive as an Obsidian vault in your backup folder: a note for every person and chat, linked together, with the messages a year at a time (a month in a busy year; a short chat in its own note) and photos shown where they were sent. **Keep them up to date after every backup and import** rewrites only what changed; anything you write below a note's "Your notes" line is kept. Each person's note is the place to start: how you talk (who sends more, who starts, how fast you each reply, when in the day and week), the moments of your chats linked to their day, the years at a glance, their photos and links, and their connections: topics you share with others, who's named in your chats and who they're in groups with. People you've never written to (codes, alerts, spam) are marked one-way and kept out of the way. The notes also read your conversations for **what happened and when**: a calendar (a note a year and a month, every day linking to it in each chat), events found in your messages (birthdays, including likely ones for contacts without one and your own, congratulations, holiday greetings, travel, meeting up, days of many photos), every photo by day, the phrases you use as topics, a timeline for each person, and who talks most in each group. **Delete the notes…** removes them all, after two confirmations.
- **Every app's chats tied to your contacts.** Turn on **Save all my contacts** in the Android app's Settings and each backup carries your whole address book; bring it into the archive and People ties chats from texts, WhatsApp, Telegram and other apps to the right contact, by number, email, or (for apps that use your phone's names) a name only one contact has. Off unless you turn it on.
- **More from your phone's contacts (Android app).** A phone backup also keeps each contact's other numbers and email addresses, nickname, company and job title, birthday, labels and relations. In the archive, chats with each of someone's numbers join into one person, and their Obsidian note shows their photo, work, birthday, labels (each a note linking everyone with it) and relations.

- **Much smaller phone backups (Android app).** Each chat's messages are compressed (text takes a tenth of the space or less: a test backup went from 6.3 MB to 0.45 MB), a photo or video that arrives more than once is stored once, and a backup kept online is cleaned of the old copies each update leaves behind. Backups are quicker too (less to write). Photos are still kept byte for byte. Backups made before keep working, and updating one adds compressed pieces. **Older versions of the app and the extension can't read the new backups**: update both.
- **No crash when the backup file is gone (Android app).** If the backup file was deleted, moved or replaced (on Google Drive, say), **Back up now** crashed the app and automatic backups failed with a technical "does not have permission" notification. Now both say the file is out of reach and to choose it again in Settings → Backup file → Change.
- **A full phone is said plainly (Android app).** "This phone ran out of space (…free now)" instead of "ENOSPC", with what to do; a full phone no longer counts photos as unreadable, the copy on the phone isn't started when the phone is short of space, and an automatic backup cut short by a full phone no longer leaves a broken file behind.
- **A copy on the phone of a backup on Google Drive (Android app).** Each backup adds to that copy and uploads it, instead of downloading the whole file from Drive first, and the app never reads the file on Drive back. Settings → *Backup online* → **Keep a copy on this phone** shows how much room it takes; turn it off to delete it. On by default; left out when the phone is short of space.
- **A new first screen (Android app).** It's organised by app, starting with **Google Messages**, ready for other apps later. The card at the top has one button (**Back up now**, or **Back up this phone** the first time), with **Back up to another file** and **Restore a backup** below it. Screens you can go back from have a back arrow and their own title; Settings is grouped the same way, with the last backup at the top and the options for a file online in their own section. A routine update that adds nothing says **Already up to date**, and the result screens make **Done** the main button.
- **Upload less with a backup on Google Drive (Android app).** Settings → **Upload less: a small updates file** keeps a second, small file beside the backup: each automatic run writes only what's new there (kilobytes, not the whole backup), and the big file catches up only when that's worth it. Restore or bring in the big file, then the updates file.

### Fixed
- A backup from Google Messages no longer forgets that a contact is starred on your phone.
- The toolbar button's tooltip said "Export Google Messages"; it says **Message Archivist**.
- Two Settings switches flipped at the same moment could lose the first change.
- Other apps' chats no longer show as "Folder missing" in Settings, nor "no phone number yet"; deleting one says it doesn't touch that app.
- The viewer's chat lists, search results and About say which app a chat is from, and the chat header shows its number formatted (and not twice).
- **The Android app no longer crashes** turning on automatic backup to a file on Google Drive (or turning on **Only on Wi-Fi**) on Android 14 and later. If Android ever refuses the schedule, the first screen says so, and opening the app puts back a schedule that went missing.
- **No whole backup made again for a file Google Drive hasn't uploaded yet (Android app).** Drive shows a new or rewritten backup as 0 bytes until its app has uploaded it, which some Wi-Fi networks hold up. The app used to take such a file for an empty one and write the whole backup again, every run; now it waits (for up to a day) and says the last backup hasn't been uploaded yet. The updates file is filled as soon as you set it up, instead of staying 0 bytes until the next run, and after a backup to a file online the app says Drive shows it as 0 bytes until it's uploaded.
- **An automatic backup to Google Drive no longer breaks itself (Android app).** If its first backup failed, the app deleted the new file it had just made, and every backup after that failed. Now the file is kept and the next backup starts it over, and the first screen says the last backup didn't work instead of still showing the one before.
- Turning on **Back up automatically** after a backup keeps your other automatic-backup settings (how often, charging, Wi-Fi, notifications) instead of resetting them.

- **Texts from more than one number stay apart.** A phone backup from a second phone or a work number keeps its own chats ("Google Messages · <number>") instead of joining your main number's chats with the same people; Settings asks once if it can't tell. Phone restore picks one number's texts at a time, and the viewer filters by source and number. The Android app's backups record the phone's own number when the phone knows it.
- **One way to bring things in.** Settings → Tools → **Bring in conversations** has **Bring in files…** and **Bring in a folder…**, which recognise a phone backup (with its updates file), another app's export, or an older export by themselves, instead of six separate buttons.
- **Plain-words errors for a backup file that can't be read**: an empty file (a Google Drive download that didn't finish), one cut short, or one that isn't a backup at all.
- **The viewer on a phone-width screen** shows the chat list, then one chat with a back arrow, instead of squeezing both.

- Settings → Backups no longer spills off the right of the window when a chat's name, handle or folder has no spaces in it; the viewer's top bar fits a narrow window too.
### Changed
- **Your archive is part of Settings.** One menu down the left has your archive (Chats, People, Contacts, Search, Media, Review, Analysis, About) and, below it, Home, Backups, Phone restore, Tools, Settings and Apps (☰ shrinks it to icons, and it remembers); the page opens on your chats once you've chosen a folder, in the app and the extension (**Open my backup** goes there too).
- **Contacts.** A new tab in the viewer shows everyone as a circle (their photo, or their initial in their colour) in a grid, with their name or number below; search and sort it, and click someone to see all your chats with them.
- **Choose how chats and people are sorted** (Settings → *Order of chats and people*): most recent message, most messages, or favorites then A–Z, for the viewer's Chats and People lists and the Backups tab, which now has its own sort menu too. Each list's menu still changes it while you look.
- Text chats are labelled **Google Messages** (they were "Text messages"), so they're told apart from iMessage's and other sources' texts.
- Other apps' chats are kept in `conversations/<App>/` in the backup folder, with the new status **Imported**; text chats stay where they are. The library's index is version 5 (older libraries read as they are).
- Phone restore, "Back up everything", photo fetching and the backup list apply to text chats only.

## [0.0.1.0] - 2026-10-27

The first public release of Message Archivist: a Chrome extension and an Android app that keep your text messages on your own devices.

### Chrome extension
- **Backs up Google Messages for Web** into one folder on your computer: one chat or every chat, every message with who sent it and when, photos at full size, GIFs, videos, voice notes, contact names, numbers and pictures. Messages are saved as they're read, so a backup that stops carries on where it left off; after that only new messages are added, by hand or on a schedule.
- **Checks every chat is complete**: that its first message was reached (and on what evidence), that the newest are saved, that nothing is duplicated and every photo is in. "Back up everything" also checks every conversation in Google Messages against the backup.
- **A master list of photos and videos** per chat, with each one's status (full size, thumbnail, not fetched, failed), so a quick run can save the messages and the photos can follow later.
- **Labels, flags and folders** for chats and single messages, and a Review tab of everything flagged.
- **Starts from a phone backup**: brings in a backup from the Android app, matching chats by phone number, then adds what the phone doesn't keep without saving anything twice. Also brings in older exports.
- **Reads anywhere**: a Google Messages-style viewer with search (quoted words match whole words), a media gallery, a timeline, and analysis (activity, a year in review, reply times, most-used words), favorite chats first and numbers formatted one way.
- **Makes a restore file** for putting a backup back on an Android phone (and an SMS Backup & Restore file).
- **Evidence-grade records**: a SHA-256 for every file, a log of every run, Google's message IDs and the on-screen timestamps.

### Android app
- **Backs up a phone's texts in minutes**: every text and picture message in every chat, group chats included, with exact times, photos and videos (or without them, to add later), contact names, favorites and contact photos, into one file on the phone or Google Drive.
- **Keeps that file up to date by itself**: every 12 hours, day or week, only while charging or on Wi-Fi if you like, adding only what's new. A status card says when the last backup ran.
- **Puts a backup back on a phone**: every chat or the ones you pick, in order, without duplicates, with photos and videos; restoring again carries on where it stopped. It becomes the SMS app only while it restores, and walks you through switching back.
- **No internet permission**: messages go only where you save the file.
- Signed with the project's key, so later versions install over this one.
